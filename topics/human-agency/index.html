A reading guide

Human agency in an AI world

As AI takes on more work, the question is not only what it can do. It is what people can still decide, question and change. These essays explore that question from regulation and design to the relationship between human intent and machine autonomy.

Agency and participation are different

Human agency is the ability to act and decide. Human-in-the-loop describes a person’s participation in a decision or process loop. The two are related, but participation alone does not establish meaningful control: the person’s role, information and ability to intervene matter.

Read the argument in order

The three-part 2021 series begins with regulation, examines how algorithms shape our choices, and turns to the design of systems that preserve human agency. The earlier essay on intent asks why a system’s objective cannot be separated from the people who define it.

From the essays to the work

Nera continues the practical thread: keeping intent, requirements and architectural decisions explicit as people and AI agents build software. The career story explains the current work; the essays retain the ideas and context of their original publication.
